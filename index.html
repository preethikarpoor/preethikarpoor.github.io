About

Hi, I'm Preethi.

I'm an astronomer obsessed with a deceptively simple question: how common are planets like Earth? I try to answer it by finding and characterizing planets around nearby M dwarf stars, and building the software pipelines that make that science possible.

My current work spans two institutions — I'm a Research Affiliate at Louisiana State University working on photometry pipelines for the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope, and a Research Associate at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics in Bengaluru exploring atmospheric characterization for directly imaged planets with SCALES on Keck.

I came to astronomy through Mechanical Engineering — which is not the most conventional path. I graduated as valedictorian from VTU in 2020, fell completely in love with telescope design while interning at IIA, and never looked back. That engineering instinct still shapes everything I build.

When I'm not doing science, I think a lot about who gets to be an astronomer and why — and I try to do something about it.

Research

What I work on

I'm an exoplanet astronomer — but that means a lot of different things depending on which method you use to find them. Here are the four corners of my research universe.

Transit Photometry
Finding planets by watching stars dim as a world passes in front of them. My EXOSCAPER pipeline does this at scale for M dwarfs in TESS data.
Explore →
Direct Imaging
Blocking out a star's blinding glare to photograph planets directly. At IIA Bengaluru, I'm building the science case for SCALES on Keck.
Explore →
Microlensing
Using gravity itself as a telescope. At LSU, I'm building photometry pipelines to prepare Roman for its microlensing survey of the Galactic Bulge.
Explore →
Demographics
Zooming out to ask: what does the whole population of exoplanets look like? I work on occurrence rates and planetary population statistics.
Explore →
