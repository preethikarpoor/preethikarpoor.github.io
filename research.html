Research

I work across exoplanet detection and demographics, survey methodology, atmospheric modeling, and instrumentation — with a through-line of building tools that make large surveys scientifically sharper and more inclusive.

Exoplanets

TESS, Roman, Keck SCALES, and occurrence-rate frameworks.

Instrumentation & Observatories

NLOT, NLST, and hardware-informed survey design.

Planetary Science

Chandrayaan-2 lunar surface science and rover concepts.

Galaxies & Machine Learning

Galaxy morphology, AGN, and transformer-based methods.

TESS & EXOSCAPER: M-dwarf Transit Detection and Demographics

I am building EXOSCAPER, a TESS transit-detection and vetting pipeline tailored to active mid-to-late M dwarfs. The pipeline integrates flare-aware light-curve preparation, multi-stage detection, and community-standard vetting tools to produce a reproducible catalog of small-planet candidates and occurrence-rate measurements.

This work draws on my Master’s thesis at UC San Diego and ongoing collaborations with IPAC/Caltech and Penn State, and is designed to scale from a carefully-curated nearby sample to larger demographic ensembles.

Roman GBTDS Photometry & Survey Methodology

With collaborators at LSU and IPAC, I am benchmarking photometric pipelines for the Roman Galactic Bulge Time-Domain Survey. I compare PRF-based and aperture photometry on simulated stacked images across cadence, crowding, and magnitude, with the goal of defining a “Roman analogue” of Kepler CDPP for small-signal exoplanet and microlensing science.

This work is driven by survey design questions: how cadence, extraction strategy, and systematics modeling propagate into detectability and yield.

Keck SCALES: Atmospheric Modeling & Target Feasibility

As a Project Associate at the Indian Institute of Astrophysics, I contribute to science planning for the Keck SCALES instrument. I work on atmospheric and spectral modeling for directly imaged planets, target list curation, and feasibility studies that connect instrumental performance to science cases in the mid-infrared.

This connects directly to my broader interest in bridging survey detection, detailed characterization, and instrument capabilities.

ISRO ExoWorld Mission: Photometric Precision & Mission Design

At ISRO HQ, I worked on precursor science for the proposed ExoWorld Mission, studying how photometric precision impacts exoplanet detection and validation using Kepler and K2 data. I implemented single- and multi-aperture pipelines for full-frame images to assess detection thresholds and inform telescope and survey design.

National Large Optical Telescope (NLOT)

I have contributed to feasibility and science-case work for the proposed NLOT, with a focus on how large-aperture optical facilities can support exoplanet and time-domain surveys. This builds on my background in mechanical engineering and my interest in connecting hardware constraints to survey design.

National Large Solar Telescope (NLST)

My work on NLST centers on understanding how solar-focused instrumentation, adaptive optics, and site conditions shape the achievable science. Although distinct from exoplanets, this work sharpens my intuition for instrumentation pipelines and precision photometry in challenging regimes.

Mechanisms, Controls & Rover Concepts

Earlier in my trajectory, I worked on mechanism design and control systems in the context of a lunar rover concept study and advanced engineering design courses. This experience continues to inform how I think about the interfaces between mechanical systems, detectors, and the data they deliver.

Chandrayaan-2: Lunar Surface & Crater Science

At the IISc–ISRO Space Astronomy Group, I worked with data from Indian lunar missions to study surface processes, crater populations, and compositional signatures. This planetary-science work complements my exoplanet focus by grounding questions of habitability and planetary environments in our own Solar System.

Lunar Rover Concept & Mobility Studies

As an undergraduate mechanical engineer, I contributed to rover-mobility and mechanism design studies for lunar surface exploration, including terrain interaction and power considerations. This project seeded my interest in space-mission hardware and still informs how I think about end-to-end experiment design.

Galaxy Morphology with Vision Transformers

As a post-baccalaureate research assistant at Caltech, I developed Vision-Transformer models for morphological classification of galaxies, exploring how attention-based architectures compare to traditional CNNs in crowded, low-signal imaging.

Transformers for TESS Light Curves & Transients

I have designed Transformer-based models — both Time-Series and Vision Transformers — to enhance detection of subtle exoplanetary signals in TESS photometry and to study transient phenomena in polarimetric datasets. This work ties together my interests in surveys, statistics, and modern machine learning.

Protogalactic Spin-up & Magnetic Fields

In earlier theoretical work, I explored the contribution of magnetic fields to protogalactic spin-up using linear perturbation theory, linking magnetohydrodynamics to early galaxy formation and angular-momentum transport. This project informs how I think about the broader cosmic context in which planetary systems form.
